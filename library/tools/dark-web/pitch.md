# Pitch

> A Tor-hidden, Twitter/X-style microblogging site (~25k users) known as a gathering point for threat actors — a place to track handles, posts and connections on the dark web.

## When to use
You are tracking a `username`/handle that may be active in cybercriminal or dark-web circles and want to see whether it posts on Pitch, what it says, and who it follows/interacts with. Useful for threat-actor attribution and for pivoting a handle seen on breach forums into a social graph.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual, over Tor)
1. Open the Tor Browser on a sandboxed/sock-puppet system and navigate to the `.onion` address above (the hidden-service address may rotate — reconfirm from a current dark-web link index if it fails to load).
2. Use the search / explore features to look up the handle or keyword.
3. Read the profile: posts, follows/followers, and trending topics can reveal aliases, contacts and interests.
4. Pivot: cross-reference discovered handles against breach data, other dark-web forums, and clearnet username tools to build attribution — never trust a single dark-web source alone.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** `username` / handle (or keyword)
- **Out:** `social-profile` (posts, follower/following links, associated handles)
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** no matching handle or empty profile means the actor isn't active here (or uses a different alias) — not proof of anything about the person.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Human-in-the-loop: viewing may be open, but interacting/searching some areas needs a (burner) account (`account-login`).
- Tor-only and hostile: expect malicious links and social-engineering; never deanonymise yourself, download attachments, or reuse a real handle.
- Hidden-service addresses change; a dead onion here means "find the current mirror," not necessarily "site gone."

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Pair with breach-forum and paste-monitoring tools: Pitch shows the actor's social/posting side, while breach indexes show the leaked data — together they strengthen attribution.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: unverified` — an anonymous Tor service with unvetted, largely criminal user content; every lead needs independent corroboration before it is treated as fact.
