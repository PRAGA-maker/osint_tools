# List of All Criminal Offences A–Z (Australia)

> A legal-reference glossary of Australian criminal offences — a decoder for charge terminology, NOT a database that returns records about a person.

## When to use
You've found a charge, conviction, or offence label against a subject (in an Australian court listing, news report, or record) and need to understand what it actually means, how serious it is, and what elements it involves. This A–Z, published by an Australian criminal-defence firm, explains offences in plain language. It resolves ambiguity so you correctly interpret a subject's criminal history — it does not itself look anyone up.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Go to https://www.criminal-lawyers.com.au/offences.
2. Find the offence term you encountered (browse A–Z or search the page).
3. Read the definition, typical penalties, and elements to gauge severity and context.
4. Treat it as jurisdiction-specific (Australia) — verify against the actual statute/state legislation for anything you'll rely on.
5. Pivot: understanding the charge helps you weigh a subject's record and decide what court/records to pull next (via the relevant Australian court portal).

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** an offence term/charge label (not a personal selector)
- **Out:** a plain-language definition, severity, and elements of the offence — reference only
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** the specific offence/jurisdiction isn't covered — consult the governing state/federal legislation directly.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Reference, not records: it returns no data about individuals — don't treat it as a lookup.
- Jurisdiction-bound (Australia) and firm-authored; confirm against primary legislation for accuracy.
- Terminology varies by state; a label may differ from the exact statutory charge.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Pairs with Australian court portals and record databases (which return the actual charges/cases) — this explains the terms those return.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: community` — competent law-firm reference content; reliable as a glossary but not an official legal source, so verify definitions against the relevant statute before acting.
