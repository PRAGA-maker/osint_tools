# Inc. 5000

> Inc. magazine's annual list of the fastest-growing US private companies — a searchable directory that ties a company to its city, industry, growth, and often its founder/CEO.

## When to use
You have an `employer-org` (a company name a subject is linked to) or a founder/executive `name`, and want quick corroboration: does this private US company actually exist and operate at scale, where is it headquartered, what industry is it in, and who founded/leads it? The Inc. 5000 (and its regional lists) is a clean, free source for that — each entry carries the company name, metro/city, industry, revenue-growth metrics, year, and frequently the founder or CEO. Good for grounding an employment claim or pivoting from a company to the people behind it.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Go to https://www.inc.com/inc5000 and use the list's search/filter.
2. Search the `employer-org` name (or filter by industry, state/metro, or year); regional lists (e.g. Inc. 5000 Regionals) cover more, smaller companies.
3. Open the company entry: read its city/metro (`address` at metro level), industry, growth rate, year founded, and leadership.
4. Note the founder/CEO `name` and headquarters city — pivot from company to person, or confirm a person's stated role.
5. Pivot: take the company + city into a state business-registry / Secretary of State search for the registered agent and officers, and the leader's name into people-search tooling.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** `employer-org` (company) or `name` (founder/executive)
- **Out:** `employer-org` (confirmed company + industry/growth), `address` (HQ metro/city), `name` (founder/CEO where listed)
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** no match — meaning the company never made an Inc. list (most companies don't), which says nothing about whether it exists; fall back to state business registries.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Human-in-the-loop: none.
- OpSec: passive — a public published ranking.
- It only covers companies that applied and qualified for a given year's list, so coverage is a thin slice of all US businesses, and location is metro-level, not a street address. Growth figures are self-reported (vetted by Inc.). Use it to corroborate and pivot, then confirm specifics in official registries.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Complements Secretary-of-State / business-registry searches: Inc. gives you the company's public profile and leadership; the registry gives the legal filing, registered agent, and officer addresses.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: community` — a reputable business publication, but rankings rest on company-supplied revenue data; treat the *existence and profile* as reliable and the *growth numbers* as self-reported.
