# Grammarly AI Detector

> A free paste-and-check tool that estimates how much of a passage looks machine-generated — a triage signal for authenticity work, not a verdict.

## When to use
You are assessing whether a piece of text — a dating/social profile bio, a product review, an email, a "witness" statement — was written by a human or generated by an LLM. A high AI-likelihood score can corroborate a suspicion that a profile is part of a bot/scam network or that a document was mass-produced, feeding into catfish or disinformation analysis.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Open https://www.grammarly.com/ai-detector.
2. Paste the text (up to ~10,000 characters) into the box, or upload a document.
3. Run the check and read the result: a percentage "resembling AI text" versus "no AI patterns found."
4. Interpret conservatively — treat >~60–70% AI as *worth further scrutiny*, not confirmation. Corroborate with other signals (reused profile photo via reverse image, account age, network overlap).
5. Sign in only if you need the Pro-tier sentence-level explanations.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** free text (no OSINT selector)
- **Out:** an AI-vs-human percentage score (no pivotable selector)
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** "no AI text patterns found" / near-0% — means the passage reads as human-written, which is *not* proof a human wrote it (short or heavily edited AI text evades detection).

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Human-in-the-loop: none, but the character cap forces you to chunk long documents.
- OpSec: **active** — pasted text goes to Grammarly. Never submit sensitive case material or a subject's private messages.
- False positives/negatives are common: non-native English and formulaic human writing can score as "AI," and lightly-edited AI text can score as "human." Never present the score as evidence.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Pairs with any reverse-image and account-metadata check — the detector questions the *text* of a suspect profile while those question the *image* and *account history*; convergence across all three is what actually indicts a fake.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: community` — a reputable vendor's tool, but AI-detection as a technique is inherently unreliable; use it as one weak indicator among several, never standalone.
