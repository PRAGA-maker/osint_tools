# transl8it!

> A free web translator for "txt speak" — turns SMS/chat abbreviations and shorthand ("l8r", "cu", "brb") into plain English and back.

## When to use
You're reading a subject's messages, posts, or chat logs dense with abbreviations and shorthand and want a quick plain-English rendering to be sure you're reading them right. A low-stakes comprehension aid; the meaning it gives is a starting interpretation, since slang is contextual and evolves faster than any static dictionary.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Open the site.
2. Paste the txt-speak/abbreviated text (or plain text to abbreviate).
3. Read the translated output.
4. Sanity-check unusual terms against current slang references — its dictionary skews older.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** text in SMS/chat abbreviation (or plain English)
- **Out:** a plain-English (or abbreviated) rendering
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** unchanged or garbled output for terms it doesn't know — newer or niche slang won't resolve; treat gaps as "look this up elsewhere," not "meaningless."

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Dictionary-based and dated; modern/regional slang and coded language will be missed or misread.
- Interpretation only — never treat its output as forensic evidence of meaning or intent.
- Passive and low-risk, but keep sensitive raw content out of third-party sites.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Pairs with a general translation service and up-to-date slang references (Urban Dictionary-style) for terms this tool's dictionary doesn't cover.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: community` — a novelty translator; useful for a quick decode, but corroborate any interpretation that matters against current slang sources and context.
