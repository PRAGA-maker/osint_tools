# NetLingo Listing of Chat Acronyms

> A dictionary of text/chat acronyms and slang — turn "SMH", "IYKYK", or a parents-should-know safety code into plain English when you're reading a subject's messages.

## When to use
Investigative material often includes chat logs, DMs, comments or SMS full of abbreviations and slang. When an acronym's meaning isn't obvious — and misreading it could change your interpretation — NetLingo's list gives a quick gloss, including the "acronyms every parent should know" set covering coded/predatory language relevant to safeguarding cases.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Open http://www.netlingo.com/acronyms.php.
2. Browse the alphabetical list or use in-page find (Ctrl-F) for the specific acronym.
3. Read the expansion(s); note when multiple meanings exist.
4. Check the "top 50" and "parents should know" sections for safety-relevant codes.
5. Pivot: a decoded phrase can change how you weight a message — corroborate meaning against context, not just the list.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** a chat/text acronym or slang term (a reference lookup, not a personal selector)
- **Out:** the expansion/meaning of the term
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** a very new, niche, or community-specific acronym may be absent or listed with a dated meaning — slang evolves; treat a single gloss as a lead.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Meanings are crowdsourced and shift over time; the same letters can mean different things in different communities — use surrounding context.
- It's a reference, not a person lookup; no OSINT footprint.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Complements Urban Dictionary and general translators — NetLingo is strong on acronyms/texting shorthand, Urban Dictionary on evolving slang, translators on foreign-language text.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: community` — an established but crowd-maintained reference; confirm a decoded meaning against the message's context before drawing conclusions.
