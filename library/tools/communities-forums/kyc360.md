# KYC360

> A professional AML/KYC news-and-knowledge community (by RiskScreen) — background and regulatory context for financial-crime research, not a subject-lookup tool.

## When to use
You are researching an entity (`employer-org`) or person in a financial-crime, sanctions, or due-diligence context and need to understand the regulatory landscape — new rules, enforcement trends, typologies, and expert guidance. KYC360 supplies the professional framing that makes raw findings meaningful (e.g. what a given sanctions regime requires, how a laundering typology works). It informs your approach; it does not return records on an individual.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Open https://www.kyc360.com/ and browse news and articles by topic (sanctions, AML, KYC, enforcement).
2. Register a free account (with a research email) for full-article access where gated.
3. Read for regulatory changes and typologies relevant to your subject's jurisdiction/sector.
4. Apply that context to interpret findings from actual screening/registry tools.
5. Pivot: guidance points you to the right authoritative sources (sanctions lists, registries) to run the entity through.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** an `employer-org`/sector/jurisdiction you need AML context for (a `name` in a due-diligence frame)
- **Out:** professional news/guidance that frames further research (context around an `employer-org`)
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** no article on your niche topic — it's editorial coverage, not a database, so gaps are normal; go to primary regulators for specifics.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- It is news/knowledge, NOT a records source — it will not tell you whether *your* subject is sanctioned; use dedicated sanctions/registry tools for that.
- Registration wall on full content; deeper RiskScreen products are paid.
- OpSec: passive; register with a research email, not a personal one.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Pairs with sanctions/PEP screeners and corporate registries — KYC360 explains the regime and typology; those return the actual hits on an entity.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: trusted` — professional editorial content from RiskScreen, an established AML firm; reliable as guidance, though (as commentary) confirm any specific regulatory requirement against the primary regulator.
