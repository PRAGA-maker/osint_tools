# Cyber Criminals Most Wanted

> A veteran internet-safety and cybercrime **awareness portal** — reference and referral, despite the name it is not a wanted-persons database.

## When to use
You need to orient on a category of online harm — cyberstalking, phishing/spam, hacking, online scams, child-safety — and want plain-language background plus pointers to where a victim reports it. Useful early in a case to understand the landscape and the right reporting channel. It does **not** list named offenders and will not return any selector, so do not treat it as a lookup tool.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Go to https://www.ccmostwanted.com/ (the front page can be picky over some clients; the topic sub-pages such as `/cyberstalking/`, `/hacking/`, `/spam/` open directly).
2. Browse to the relevant topic section.
3. Read the explainer and follow its outbound links to laws, expert contacts, and official reporting bodies (e.g. FBI/IC3 for US cybercrime).
4. Pivot: use the referral to file a report through the proper authority, or to understand terminology before a records search elsewhere.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** a cybercrime topic (no personal selector)
- **Out:** educational articles, safety guidance, and referral links to law-enforcement / help resources
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** any attempt to search for a specific person — the site holds no offender directory and will surface only general articles.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- **Name is misleading:** despite "Most Wanted," there is no searchable list of criminals — it is an awareness/referral site.
- The operator states plainly it is not law enforcement and cannot act on complaints; use its links to reach a real authority.
- Content skews US-centric and can be dated; verify any specific legal claim against a primary source.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Complements official channels like the FBI Cyber Most Wanted and IC3 — this explains the crime type, and those handle the actual report or list genuine wanted subjects.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: unverified` — a long-standing volunteer awareness project, not an institution; its explainers are a reasonable starting point but should be corroborated against government or legal primary sources before you rely on them.
