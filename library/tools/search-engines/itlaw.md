# The IT Law Wiki

> A crowd-sourced reference encyclopedia of information-technology and cyber law — a definitions lookup for the legal/technical jargon that shows up in records, not a source of person data.

## When to use
Not a people tool. Use it when an investigation surfaces unfamiliar IT/cyber/privacy/telecom-law terminology — a statute name, a legal-technical concept, an agency acronym — and you need a quick plain-language definition to interpret a document, subpoena, policy, or news article correctly. It sharpens your understanding of the legal context around data you've already found.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Open https://itlaw.fandom.com/ (old itlaw.wikia.com links redirect here) and search the term.
2. Read the entry for the definition, context, and any cited statutes/cases.
3. Follow citations to primary sources when the point matters legally.
4. Pivot: the correct terminology improves your searches of primary legal databases and helps you frame records requests accurately.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** a legal/technical term (reference query)
- **Out:** an explanatory definition/context entry (no person-level data)
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** no article, or a stub — the term isn't covered; go to a primary legal source or a general legal dictionary.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Crowd-edited reference — good for orientation, but never cite it as legal authority; confirm against statutes/case law.
- Coverage is broad but uneven; niche or very recent terms may be missing.
- OpSec: passive reading only.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Complements primary legal databases and general reference wikis — use this to decode a term, those to establish the authoritative legal position.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: community` — a substantial crowd-sourced reference; reliable enough for definitions and orientation, but verify legal specifics against primary sources.
