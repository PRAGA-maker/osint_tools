# AmIReal

> A browser overlay that exploits GAN face-generators' fixed eye/mouth placement to flag likely synthetic faces — a fast first check on a suspicious profile picture.

## When to use
You have a face `image` — typically a profile photo on a dating/social account you suspect is a catfish or bot — and want a quick heuristic on whether it was machine-generated by a ThisPersonDoesNotExist-style GAN. Useful early when deciding whether a "person" is even real before investing in deeper pivots.

## How to use it (`bestInteractionPattern`: web-manual)
1. Open https://seintpl.github.io/AmIReal/.
2. Load the suspect face image and overlay the adjustable alignment template.
3. Check the tells: GAN faces put the eyes (and usually the mouth) in almost the same fixed positions every time, and often show warped accessories, teeth, ears, or backgrounds.
4. Treat a match as *suspicion*, not proof — corroborate with reverse-image search and a dedicated deepfake detector before concluding.

## Inputs → Outputs
- **In:** a face `image`
- **Out:** a manual human judgment ("likely GAN-generated" vs "plausibly real")
- **Empty/negative result looks like:** eyes/mouth do NOT sit on the template and the image has consistent, natural detail — weak evidence it is a real photo, not a guarantee.

## Gotchas & OpSec
- Human-in-the-loop: **manual review** — you interpret the overlay; there is no automated score.
- OpSec: passive and client-side; nothing leaves your browser.
- Heuristic only: newer generators vary composition and defeat the fixed-position tell — a "pass" here does not mean the image is authentic.

## Overlaps ("do both")
- Pairs with reverse-image search and `[[namint]]`/`[[imagston]]` — this flags *synthetic* faces, reverse-image search finds *reused real* faces; run both to separate fakes from stolen photos.

## Trust & verifiability
`trust: community` — a well-intentioned heuristic aid from a known OSINT trainer; the authors themselves caution it is fallible, so never rely on it alone.
